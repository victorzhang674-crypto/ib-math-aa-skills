# IB Mathematics AA Paper 2

The first-assessment-2021 format has compulsory short responses in Section A and compulsory extended responses in Section B. Each section is about half the marks. An approved graphic display calculator (GDC) and a clean AA formula booklet are available. Some parts are best done analytically.

| Level | Time | Raw marks | Course weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SL | 90 minutes | 80 | 40% |
| HL | 120 minutes | 110 | 30% |

For an exam session from May 2029 onward, verify the applicable format and syllabus before grading or generating a mock. [IB curriculum update](https://www.ibo.org/university-admission/latest-curriculum-updates/dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-updates/)

## Mark a response

1. Match the question and mark scheme by course, level, session, version or time zone, and paper number. Read the complete question, diagram and student working. If the scheme is unavailable, solve independently and label any score a **practice estimate**, with the point allocation visible. Treat missing or unreadable material as unassessed. Mark confirmed blanks zero.
2. Use the matched examiner instructions and grade each subpart. M credits method, A accuracy or an answer, and R reasoning. AG denotes a result supplied in the prompt. Apply dependencies and award implied marks only as permitted. Keep multi-mark blocks together unless the scheme permits splitting. Accept mathematically equivalent answers and valid alternative routes within the question's constraints. Apply follow-through (FT), misread (MR), and accuracy rules from the scheme.
3. Check the model, equations or probability statement before evaluating the GDC result. Look for parameters, bounds, angle mode, domain and units where relevant. Accept bare answers where the scheme permits them and require visible working where it does. For “show that” or “justify,” check the reasoning leading to the claim. For “hence,” check use of the prior work. Check the paper's precision rules, typically exact or three significant figures unless the question specifies otherwise. Keep intermediate precision and assess final notation under the matched instructions.
4. Return **part | marks earned/available | evidence and missing step**. Add only assessed parts and specify what was unavailable. For repeated losses, separate model setup, GDC operation, and communication or interpretation. Give targeted revisions and a comparable retry problem. Use session-specific boundaries if the user requests a grade estimate.

Original marking illustration. In a 3-mark practice item asking for all roots of \(e^{-x}=x/2\) on \([0,2]\) and a justification of completeness, one possible allocation is M1 for setting up the intersection, A1 for \(x=0.853\) to three significant figures, and R1 for showing \(e^{-x}-x/2\) is strictly decreasing and changes sign on the interval. A screenshot of a single crossing does not establish uniqueness.

## Write solutions, practice questions, or plans

- Model a solution as mathematical setup → calculator operation or analytic steps → result in mathematical notation → interpretation when requested. Describe the operation and inputs so the student can reproduce it. Give model-specific buttons only when the model is known and the instructions are verified.
- For an original mock, mix analytical and GDC-suited parts at the selected level. Cover varied topics in Section A and use connected, progressively harder tasks in Section B. Check that required computations are feasible on an approved GDC. Supply worked solutions and a subpart mark scheme with dependencies or follow-through where applicable.
- Build timed practice around the student's marks lost. For example, have the student set up several probability or intersection problems by hand, evaluate them on the GDC, then repeat with a timed set that requires checking domains and interpreting outputs. Base whole-paper time checkpoints on the marks printed on the paper.
