# IB Mathematics AA Internal Assessment

In the first-assessment-2021 format, the IA is an individual written mathematical exploration, worth 20% at both SL and HL. The teacher marks it and the IB moderates it. The guide suggests about 12–20 double-spaced pages including diagrams and graphs, with the bibliography excluded. The cover shows the title and page count. A concise, mathematically clear report matters more than filling pages.

From May 2029, the IB uses a different set of IA criteria. Use the criteria for the student's assessment session. [IB curriculum update](https://www.ibo.org/university-admission/latest-curriculum-updates/dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-updates/)

## Help the student develop and write the exploration

Read the [writing and planning guide](references/writing.md) for planning a topic, structuring a draft, or diagnosing a mathematical approach. Start with the student's level, assessment session, interests, existing work, data access and deadline as relevant. Infer what the materials establish. With limited context, provide a conditional plan and identify the choice that matters most.

- Turn an interest into a focused mathematical aim with a testable or derivable result and a clear scope. Check feasibility with a small calculation, proof attempt or data sample before committing.
- Develop an argument the student can explain. For each stage, specify the mathematical task, the evidence it should produce and how it answers the aim. Distinguish assumptions from derived results and choose a check that could reveal when the approach fails.
- Keep authorship with the student. Offer outlines, feedback, questions, and short illustrative examples using a separate topic or clearly labeled synthetic data. Identify errors and suggest how the student can investigate them. Leave the final prose, analysis and decisions to the student. Require source credit for borrowed data, ideas, formulas, graphics or code, and follow the school's guidance on acknowledging AI assistance.

## Evaluate a draft

1. Read the [rubric and marking guide](references/rubric.md). Review the draft, including relevant figures, formulas, data and appendices when available. Recompute representative steps and test the most consequential claim. Check how the student moves from the aim to the conclusion.
2. Apply best fit independently for A through E. Use whole marks. Use the correct SL or HL scale for E. If the level is unknown, give A–D feedback and leave E conditional. For a proposal or incomplete draft, distinguish demonstrated evidence from planned work and leave unassessable criteria pending.
3. Return **criterion | provisional mark/max | evidence and band rationale | revision action**. Cite page, figure, or section anchors when available. Explain the closest higher band where useful. Give a provisional total out of 20 only when all five criteria can be assessed.
4. Prioritize the two or three changes that would most improve the exploration. Tie each to a concrete decision or revision the student can make. Where a core mathematical claim is invalid, address that before polishing the presentation.
