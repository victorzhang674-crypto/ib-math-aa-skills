# AA exploration rubric for the 2021 assessment model

Use this independent summary alongside the applicable IB guide. Criteria A–D apply to both levels. Criterion E has different descriptors at SL and HL. Maxima are A /4, B /4, C /3, D /3 and E /6, totaling /20. For any criterion, 0 means the available work falls below the first described level. An unreadable or missing part of a draft can make a criterion unassessable.

Choose the best-fit band for each criterion using evidence across the work. For mixed evidence, compare adjacent bands and choose the one that reflects the overall quality for that criterion. Treat plans as future work. Distinguish the quality of the presentation, notation, choices, evaluation and mathematics so a single strength does not inflate every score.

| Criterion | Band distinctions | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A · Presentation** | **1** Some logical flow or some organization. **2** Some of both. **3** Clear, well-structured progression. **4** The same with unnecessary material trimmed. | The introduction identifies an aim, the sections build toward it, and the conclusion resolves it. Figures sit where their evidence is used. Bulk data and supplementary outputs may go in appendices. |
| **B · Mathematical communication** | **1** Some relevant communication with partly suitable notation or representations. **2** Some relevant and suitable communication. **3** Relevant and suitable communication is mostly consistent. **4** It is consistent throughout. | Definitions, units, notation, labels, scales, and readable reasoning. A single well-chosen representation can suffice. Minor slips that leave communication clear need not prevent the top mark. |
| **C · Personal engagement** | **1** Some visible independent contribution. **2** Several authentic choices or insights move the investigation forward. **3** Numerous strong instances steer the exploration creatively and reveal the student's understanding. | A student-generated conjecture, justified choice, test, change of strategy, or comparison that shapes the mathematics. Personal anecdotes or evidence of effort alone carry little weight. |
| **D · Reflection** | **1** Limited review, often a description of results. **2** Meaningful evaluation linked to the aim, assumptions, learning or method choice. **3** Substantial critical evaluation that changes how the results are understood or how the inquiry develops. | Consequences of limitations, trade-offs, interpretation of surprising results or justified refinements. Critical reflection can appear throughout or in a sufficiently developed conclusion that shows how it shaped the exploration. |

## Criterion E · Use of mathematics

Judge relevance to the aim, demand relative to the course, correctness, and understanding evidenced by the student. One well-explored topic can support a high mark. A correct answer, formula substitution, or software output alone does not demonstrate understanding. Verify explanations, mathematical links and reasoning around the results. Occasional minor slips can be compatible with correctness if they do not undermine the reasoning or conclusions.

| Mark | Standard level | Higher level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Some pertinent mathematics appears. | Some pertinent mathematics with limited demonstrated understanding. |
| **2** | Pertinent mathematics with limited demonstrated understanding. | Some relevant mathematics is used. The mathematics is partly correct, and the student shows some knowledge and understanding. |
| **3** | Mathematics appropriate to the course level advances the aim. Understanding is limited. | Appropriate course-level mathematics is relevant and correct. The student shows some understanding. |
| **4** | Relevant course-level mathematics is partly correct. The student shows some knowledge and understanding. | Relevant course-level mathematics is correct. The student demonstrates good understanding. |
| **5** | Relevant course-level mathematics is mostly correct. The student demonstrates good understanding. | Relevant course-level mathematics is correct. Thorough understanding is evident, with sophistication or rigour. |
| **6** | Relevant course-level mathematics is correct. Thorough understanding is evident throughout. | Relevant course-level mathematics is precise, sophisticated and rigorous, with thorough understanding throughout. |

At HL, **sophistication** may come from demanding concepts, a deeper use of familiar ideas, or connections among approaches that advance the aim. **Rigour** requires clearly justified claims and logically valid steps. **Precision** requires correct mathematics and an appropriate degree of accuracy throughout. Material beyond the syllabus is optional. At either level, mathematics drawn exclusively from prior learning cannot satisfy the course-level bands.

## Calibrate feedback

- A and B can diverge. A neatly organized investigation with undefined variables can be strong on presentation and weaker in communication.
- C and D can diverge. Designing a comparison shows engagement. Explaining why the comparison changes confidence in the conclusion shows reflection.
- Evaluate E under the selected level. An appropriate SL model explained thoroughly can score well at SL. The same work needs separate evidence of demand and depth to meet higher HL bands.
- Evidence-based feedback example. “D 2/3 provisionally. Figure 4's residual pattern led you to question the model's fit, which addresses the aim. To deepen the evaluation, test whether removing the edge observations changes the estimated optimum and explain which conclusions survive.”
