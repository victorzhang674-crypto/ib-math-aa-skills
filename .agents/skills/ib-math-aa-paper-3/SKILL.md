# IB Mathematics AA HL Paper 3

Paper 3 is HL only. The first-assessment-2021 format has two compulsory extended problem-solving questions totaling 55 marks and worth 20% of the course. Each question typically develops one theme from accessible syllabus work toward a generalization or an interpretation. An approved graphic display calculator (GDC) and a clean AA formula booklet are available.

Use the duration printed on the practice paper or the schedule for the target exam session. The attached 2021 guide and original specimen specify 60 minutes. The IB's May 2026 schedule specifies 75 minutes, consistent with the IB's description of the format preceding May 2029. For May 2029 onward, verify the new syllabus and assessment model, which specifies 60 minutes and 50 marks for Paper 3. ## Mark a response

1. Match the paper and mark scheme by course, HL, session, version or time zone, and question. Get the full prompt, diagrams and legible working. When the scheme is missing, solve the question independently and give a **practice estimate** with an explicit point breakdown. Exclude missing or unreadable work from the assessed subtotal. Mark confirmed blanks zero.
2. Trace which results each subpart uses. Distinguish a new mistake from the correct use of an earlier wrong result. Apply follow-through (FT) only where the matched scheme permits it, with its stated conditions. When a result is given in a “show that” part, the student can use that result in a later part even if their derivation failed. Score the later work on its own evidence under the scheme. Check any “hence” requirement and accept an alternative where allowed.
3. Award each mark using the matched scheme. M credits method, A accuracy or an answer, and R reasoning. AG identifies a result given in the question. Respect dependencies, implied marks, multi-mark blocks and any misread or precision instructions. Check the GDC setup where working is required. A numerical pattern can support a conjecture, while a proof or generalization requiring justification needs a valid argument over the stated domain. Check whether the final interpretation answers the question and respects the context.
4. Report **part | marks earned/available | evidence | dependency or next fix**. State which credit was follow-through. Give the assessed subtotal and identify the earliest fix that would recover the most marks. For a partial script, use the assessed maximum and identify the missing work.

Original marking illustration. If a student obtains the wrong parameter in (a) and uses it correctly to solve the equation in (b), check whether (b) explicitly permits their parameter and whether it still yields an admissible problem. Credit only the follow-through allowed by the scheme. If (c) asks for a proof, evaluate the argument independently of the quality of the earlier arithmetic.

## Write solutions, practice questions, or plans

- In worked solutions, name the result carried from each earlier part and explain why it applies. Make the shift from special cases to a general argument explicit. If interpreting a result, state its meaning and range of validity in context.
- For a practice paper, build two original investigations. Each should have an accessible entry point, linked steps, and a substantial conclusion, such as a justified general formula or a model interpretation. Give intermediate targets where useful so one error need not block an entire question. Solve every part independently and with its intended dependencies. Supply a point-by-point scheme that distinguishes method, accuracy and reasoning and specifies permitted follow-through.
- For a timed plan, divide time by question marks and keep a review buffer. Practise writing a brief map of subpart dependencies, moving past a blocked part, and returning to justify the final result. When the level is SL, offer a Paper 1 or Paper 2 plan as appropriate.
